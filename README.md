# My Bookshelf

A self-contained visual bookshelf app: `index.html` plus its `css/`, `js/` and `assets/` folders. Open `index.html` in a browser — no build step, no server, no install. Keep the folders next to `index.html`; if you move or copy the app, move the whole folder. It also works hosted on GitHub Pages.

## Code layout

Plain files, no build step. The scripts are classic `<script>` tags (not modules, which won't load from a file), so they share globals and **load in the order `index.html` lists them**, with `js/main.js` last since it starts the app. The stylesheets are also order-sensitive: later rules win ties, so keep them in the listed order.

- `css/`: `theme-colors` (every theme's colours, light and dark) → `theme-styles` (each theme's panels, shelves, buttons, fonts) → `base` (page, shelves, books, windows) → `effects` (sparkles, exits) → `form` (edit window) → `shelf-extras` (leaning, sticky notes, dust) → `corner` (bottom-left stack, debug panel) → `windows` (goals, critter, stats, library card) → `pins` (pin board) → `slips` (Slips window) → `cat` → `phone`
- `js/`: `settings` (constants, Settings, genres, theme, mode) · `db` (IndexedDB, starter books, backups) · `migrations` (one-time data fixes) · `fx` (sparkles, confetti) · `shelf` (rendering, drag & drop, leaning, dust, cat, filters) · `sticky` (sticky notes) · `form` (edit window, tags, dates, notes, crop) · `critter` · `goals` · `stats` · `pins` (pin board) · `slips` (Slips window) · `debug` · `exits` (deleted books and notes leaving) · `main` (`init()`)

## Data storage

**The short version:** your books save automatically, all the time — there's no save button, you don't have to do anything. Closing the browser, shutting down your computer, whatever — none of that loses anything. Come back anytime and your books are still there. You only ever need the Export/Import buttons if you want to copy your books onto a *different* device or browser (like putting them on a tablet too). Moving or renaming `index.html` (or its folder) can make some browsers treat it as a new site with an empty shelf, so export a backup first; Import brings everything back.

The longer, more technical version:

All data lives **locally in your browser**, not on a server:

- **IndexedDB** (`BookShelfDB`) stores the actual library — titles, authors, page counts, cover/spine images, status, ratings, start/finish dates and any re-reads (`extraReads`), tags, notes, who a book is lent to and since when (`lentTo` / `lentAt`), who recommended it (`recommendedBy`), and when each book entered its current status or was last dusted off (`statusSince` / `dustedAt`, used for the dust effect).
- **localStorage** stores a few small things: your custom genre list, recent colors, which shelves are expanded, your last-used notes font, the last genre picked on a sticky note, your Settings choices, the pins you've earned, your recs from friends, when you last exported a backup from this browser (for the backup reminder), and a few one-time migration flags.

There is no backend and no account — the app makes no network calls of its own (the only external request is loading the Google Fonts used for styling).

What this means in practice:

- Your library is tied to **that specific browser on that specific device**. A copy of the app opened on your tablet has its own separate library from the one on your desktop — nothing syncs automatically between them.
- Clearing site data/cache, switching browsers, or opening the file in a private/incognito window gives you a fresh library, not your existing books.
- **First visit:** on the hosted site, a new visitor starts with an example shelf (`starter-library.json`, a real shelf with notes, dates and ratings left out) so they can see how everything works. A popup offers to clear those example books, and the pins they earned, straight away (handy for a friend starting their own shelf from a copy of yours); **Settings > Start over > Remove example shelf** does the same later, keeping any books they've added since. Opened straight from a file, the browser can't load that, so it starts with 3 placeholder books instead.
- **Export Library (Backup)** / **Import Library** (buttons above the shelves) are the only way to move your library between devices, or to back it up. Backups include your Settings too (theme and its colour, mode, name, reading goal, fonts, extras, earned pins, recs), and importing one restores them. Since there's no cloud sync, it's worth exporting a backup periodically if this data matters to you long-term.
- The app asks the browser to keep its storage for good, so the shelf isn't cleared when the disk runs low or you haven't visited in a while. Browsers may still say no, so keep exporting.
- If the shelf is just the 3 placeholder books, Export warns you first (your real books are probably in another browser or copy of the app), and the backup reminder stays quiet.
- Import **replaces** your current library entirely with whatever's in the file you pick — there's a confirmation prompt since this can't be undone.
- **Backup reminder:** if you haven't exported in 30 days (or a week after you first opened the app, if you never have), a note pops up under the Export button with **Back up now** and **Remind me later** (asks again in a week). While your last backup is over a week old, the Export button also glows softly every ten minutes or so. Exporting from either Export button counts. Turn both off with Settings > Backups, which also shows when you last backed up. The reminder is per browser and isn't saved in backups.

## Features

### Organizing your shelves
- Organize by Genre, Reading Status, Author, Series, Tag, or Rating; filter by status, ownership (All / Owned / Not Owned / Lent Out), genre, or tag; search by title/author.
- **Sticky Notes filter:** Show the to-get notes mixed in with your books, Hide them, or show Only them (a shopping list). Remembered between visits.
- Sort by Custom (drag & drop), Author last name, or Rating (high to low).
- Two view modes: **Covers** (front-cover art) and **Spines** (a shelf-accurate spine view — generated leather/gold look by default, or upload a real photo of the spine per book).
- Drag-and-drop manual reordering (in Genre, Reading Status, and Rating views, when Sort is set to Custom) — persists across reloads. Dragging a book onto another shelf moves it there: a new genre, a new status, or a new star rating. In Rating view every star level gets a shelf, even empty ones, so you can always drag a book to re-rate it.
- Long shelves can be expanded into multiple rows with the little round chevron button under them (it flips to collapse) instead of scrolling sideways. Each shelf remembers whether it's expanded.
- Each shelf shows a faint count of its books in the top-right corner.
- **Shelves act like real shelves:**
  - **Pull out to open:** clicking a book slides it out toward you before the edit window opens, and Cancel slides it back. Dragging still reorders like before, because a drag never counts as a click.
  - **Leaning books:** when a shelf has room left over, its last book leans over onto its neighbour. Adding or removing books makes it straighten up or tip over.
  - **Dust:** books that have sat in **Want to Read** for 3+ months gather a little dust, and more after 6 months. Hover over a dusty book (or tap it on a phone) to blow the dust off. That counts as dusting, so it stays clean and slowly builds up again.
  - **Lent-out books:** a book you've lent keeps its spot, but as an empty dashed outline with a faint ghost of the cover and a tall cream gift tag like on a Christmas present (top corners clipped off, a punched hole) swinging on a plain twine string inside it; long names wrap onto more lines so the tag stays tall: "lent to Sam" (written sideways in Spines view). Hover for how long it's been gone. The empty spot gathers dust after a month away, and more after three; blowing it off counts as a nudge, so it builds up again from then. Lent books don't lean, and nothing leans on them.
  - **Shelf cat:** a little black pixel cat naps on top of a shelf. Hover over it (or tap it) and it wakes up, stretches, and hops over to another shelf.
  - **Deleting a book:** the book leaves the shelf in style (sticky notes have their own ways, below). If it was pulled out, it slides back into its spot first, then goes one of seven ways at random:
    - **Tip:** rocks back, tips forward off the ledge toward you and drops, with a puff of dust
    - **Domino:** topples into its neighbour, the next few books wobble down the row, then it slips down out of sight between them
    - **Fling:** pulled out toward you, a wind-up, then thrown end over end off the side of the screen
    - **Shred:** a shredder pops up on the ledge; the book is fed in, rattling, and comes out underneath as strips that flutter away
    - **Burn:** lit at the top, it burns down like a match with a glowing ember edge, smoke and sparks, leaving a little pile of ash that blows away
    - **Pages:** the cover swings open and full-size pages burst out, flapping away like a flock of birds
    - **Trapdoor:** a hole opens under it; it hangs in the air a beat like a cartoon, then drops straight through

    Some themes have an exit of their own, used half the time:
    - **Terminal:** the book glitches (jumping, splitting into red and green ghosts), then switches off like an old CRT (a line, a dot, gone), while `> rm "Title"` and `deleted.` type themselves out above it
    - **Holo:** a rainbow scanline sweeps over it, it shivers into colour-shifting holographic slices, and they blink out one by one with a burst of sparkles
    - **Blueprint:** it fades back to a dashed drawing with its width dimensioned underneath, and a pink eraser rubs it out, dropping crumbs
    - **Library:** a red WITHDRAWN stamp slams onto the cover, then the book is slid up off the shelf and away
    - **Deco:** a sweep of gold leaf, then it opens out like a fan, snaps shut into a thin gilt stick and sinks away in a glint
    - **Typewriter:** it's typed over with rows of red X's (jolting with each key), a `ding!` pops up, and the carriage return sends it sliding off the side
    - **Sunset:** a glowing horizon appears under it and it sinks below it like the setting sun, glowing gold, then red, then plum, with embers rising

    The books next to it wait until it's gone, then slide over to fill the gap. With Calm mode (or your device's reduced-motion setting) on, the book simply disappears.
- **Reading stats:** click the little ledger book in the bottom-left corner for your total number of books, counts by status, owned and lent-out books, average rating, pages read, and a chart of books finished each month (pick any year; hover a column to see the titles, or switch to a table). The chart's subtitle also totals that year's pages. Books you finished with only a year set are counted separately. Re-reads written on a book's library card count as another book finished (and their pages count again), in the chart and toward the yearly goal.
- **Sticky notes (books you want to get):**
  - **Quick add:** click the pile of sticky notes in the bottom-left corner, jot down a title, author and genre, and "Stick it ✓" puts the book straight onto your Want to Read list. The pile crumples the note and tosses it onto the shelf.
  - **On the shelf:** the book shows up as a yellow sticky note written in pencil (Nanum Pen Script), stuck on at a slightly crooked angle; some have a curled-up corner. Click it to open and edit it like any book.
  - **Falling off:** now and then a note comes unstuck and falls to the bottom of its shelf. Click it to stick it back up.
  - **Turning into a real book:** a sticky note becomes a regular book when you save it with any of these:
    - Owned ticked
    - a cover image added
    - a spine image added
    - a new cover color
    - a new width
    - a new cover shape (ratio preset)

    The sticky note takes off (one of the ways below) and the real book poofs into its spot in a little cloud with sparkles. Editing only the title, author, genre, status, rating, tags, dates or notes keeps it a sticky note. A note that became a book only because Owned was ticked turns back into a sticky note if you untick Owned. Once you've restyled it, it stays a book.
  - **Deleting one:** the note leaves in style, one of four ways at random:
    - crumpled into a pointy paper ball and thrown off the screen
    - folded into a paper airplane that swoops away
    - crumpled and dropped: it bounces on the bottom edge, sits a moment, then fades or rolls away
    - set on fire, smouldering away from the bottom up like a cigarette, with a glowing ember edge, wisps of smoke and falling ash

    It heads off a random side of the screen each time. The books next to it wait until it's gone, then slide over to fill the gap.
  - **Filter:** use the Sticky Notes buttons (Show / Hide / Only) to mix them in with your books, hide them, or show just them (a shopping list).
  - **Settings:** Settings can hide the sticky-note pile. With Calm mode (or your device's reduced-motion setting) on, notes simply appear and disappear without the animations.
- **Reading goals card:** a little card in the bottom-left stack, on top of the stats ledger, with the critter sitting on top of it. It shows your yearly books goal (if you've set one) and your pages goal for today, this week or this month, each with a progress bar. Big numbers are shortened to fit (1,250 shows as 1.2k, rounded down so a goal never looks met early); hover for the exact numbers. Click it to open the **Reading Goals** window:
  - **Yearly reading goal:** counts books marked Read with a finish date this year (0 turns it off). The progress bar also shows in the Reading Stats window.
  - **Pages goal:** a number of pages **per day, per week or per month** (20 a day by default). Weeks start on Monday, and months on the 1st. Pages count toward it when you feed the critter, or when you type them in the window's **Log** box. The Log box also feeds the critter if it's around, so you never need to enter pages twice. Logging still works with the critter turned off. The Log box has a day picker (today by default) for pages read on an earlier day, back about five weeks: they count toward that day, week or month (the critter still eats them now). The last 40 days of pages are kept, and they're saved in backups.
  - **Candy and Blueprint** dress the window up: each goal gets its own colour (bubblegum and mint; orange and cobalt) and a big ring with the count in it, the bars are fat and striped, a line under the pages bar says what's left ("7 pages to go today", or "175 to go · about 30 a day for 6 days"), and a **Last 7 days** strip shows each day's pages against the daily pace (hover a bar for the count). The other themes keep the plain text and bars.
- **Reading critter:** a little pet you keep alive by reading, at the top of the bottom-left stack: it sits on the goals card, which sits on the stats ledger, which sits on the sticky-note pile. Click it to open its care window.
  - **Feeding:** type how many pages you read (or tap +10 / +25 / +50) and press Feed. Feeding works on the honor system: there's no check that you really read them.
    - **Limits:** up to 2,000 pages per feed, with no limit on how many times you feed. Its belly holds up to **3 days of food**, at your pages goal's pace. A weekly or monthly goal is spread out per day: 70 a week is 10 pages a day, and a month counts as 30 days. Pages over the belly's limit don't fill it, but they still count toward growing up (and toward your pages goal).
    - **Hunger:** the belly empties steadily at the pages goal's pace, in real time, even while the page is closed. It doesn't get hungry while it's an egg, after it has run away, or while it's switched off in Settings. Changing the pages goal changes the pace from then on.
    - **A newborn** starts with at least a day's food, however small its first meal.
  - **What feeding gets you:**
    - **Hatching** the egg (on its first meal).
    - **Growing up**, by pages eaten: baby, kid (150), teen (600, gets an extra like ears, claws or glasses) and adult (1,500, gets a crown piece like sprinkles, a starfish or a graduation cap). Each new stage is a little bigger, with confetti.
    - **A happier critter:** stuffed (2+ days of food), content (1–2 days), hungry (under a day; a "!" bubble on the corner widget) and starving (empty; "!!"). Between 11pm and 7am it's asleep, unless it's hungry or you're up reading too: feeding it keeps it awake for two hours, and poking it twice wakes it for an hour. Its face and what it says when you pet it (click it in the window) follow its mood.
    - **Not running away**: see below.
  - **Running away:** starve it for 7 days and it leaves a note, scrawled in blue crayon in a wobbly kid's hand (kid spelling, every letter tipped its own way, signed with a paw print). The warnings beforehand: a "!" bubble once it has under a day of food left, then "!!" once its belly is empty. While it's starving, its window and the corner widget's tooltip count down ("Starving! It might run away in 3 days."). There are no notifications outside the page. Hatch a new egg to start again; the old one goes in the **Scrapbook** with its name, stage, pages eaten and dates.
  - **Theme critters:** each theme has its own critter and home: a gummy blob in a candy jar (Candy), a hermit crab in a sea-glass bottle (Sea Glass), a little green alien in a holographic egg toy (Holo), a bookworm in a hollowed-out book (Library), a songbird in a brass birdcage (Deco), a moss sprout in a terrarium (Loft), a line-drawn pup in a drafted box (Blueprint; a collar and tag as a teen, a hard hat as an adult), a pixel pet on a little LCD (Terminal), an ink drop in an inkwell (Typewriter; a curl of ink as a teen, a quill as an adult), a little sun in a porthole onto a sunset sky (Sunset; rays as a teen, heart sunglasses as an adult) and a flat cat on a cushion in a plain room, drawn in the picked colour (Plain and Plain Bold; a collar and bell as a teen, round glasses as an adult). Give it a name (click the name in its window, type, and press Enter), or it goes by each theme's critter name.
  - **Settings:** Shelf extras can hide it; while it's off, it doesn't get hungry. It's saved in backups.
- **Pin board** (Candy and Blueprint for now): a little corkboard in a candy-pink frame hanging by a string from a brass nail in the bottom-left stack, under the critter and above the goals card, with your three newest enamel pins on it and a "+2" badge for pins you haven't looked at yet. Settings > Shelf extras > **Pin board** switches it off (the critter moves down into its spot). Click it and the big board pops up over the page on its own (no window round it), hung on a nail like the little one; close it with its round × on the corner, a click outside it, or Esc. Every pin is on it, no labels, stuck in by hand all over the cork at their own angles, spread evenly without lining up in rows and never overlapping (on a small board with lots of pins, they get a little smaller instead; the same spots every time), the ones still to earn just a flat shadow of their shape on the cork. Hover over a pin (or a shadow) and a paper card pops up right by it with its name, what it's for, and when you got it (or how far along you are, like "3 of 10 so far"). Click a pin to keep its card up; click it again, or the cork, to put it away. The first time you open it after earning new pins, it gives you a second to look, then pushes them in one by one onto their shadows (which wait in their spots till then): dropped in at a slant with their shadow on the cork closing in as they fall, pressed down with a wobble and a few sparkles, then a gleam of light runs across the enamel once each has settled. Then the first new one's card pops up.
  - **The pins:** First Chapter (first book read), Bookworm (10), Full Shelf (50), Century (100), Five Stars (a 5-star rating), Box Set (every book in a series read; needs "Out of" set), Second Helping (a re-read on the library card), Doorstopper (a 500+ page book), Speed Reader (finished within 3 days, full dates), Halfway There (halfway to the yearly goal), Goal!   - **The look:** hard-enamel pins in gold or silver. The metal is drawn with sharp light and dark bands like polished metal, and a glint that flashes across now and then (not in Calm mode); the enamel has a crisp glassy shine. Each sits on the cork at its own slight tilt with a shadow underneath.
  - **Blueprint:** the same corkboard on a nail, in a cobalt-blue frame. The enamel takes Blueprint's colours (signal orange, cobalt, steel blue and paper white), and the card is hand-lettered.
  - Pins are earned in every theme, saved in backups, and stay once earned even if the books that earned them go. Only books you can see count.
- **Slips** (button next to Settings): three slips of paper in one window. Each title opens the book.
  - **Lent Out:** a library date-due slip listing every book you've lent, who has it and how long it's been, longest gone first. Over a month gets a red OVERDUE stamp. **back ✓** marks it returned.

- **On phones:** Search stays at the top and everything else folds into a **Filters & sorting** button (it shows a count like "• 2" when filters are on); button groups wrap neatly onto new lines. Books are drawn a bit smaller so more fit per row, and the edit window shows a compact preview row at the top.

### Book details
- **Ratings:** 1–5 stars per book (click the current star again to clear it).
- **Pages:** how many pages the book has (under Details). Shows on the library card and adds up in Reading stats.
- **Recommended by:** who told you to read it (under Details). The book shows up on the From Friends slip.
- **Lending:** once **I Own This Book** is on, a **Lent to** box appears. Type who has it and save; the hint says how long ago it went out, and **it's back ✓** clears it. Changing the name starts the clock again. Unticking Owned clears it.
- **Reading dates:** Started and Finished dates under Status. A full date, a month and year, or just a year all work, and each has a **today** button. The app catches impossible dates and a finish date before the start date. Dates are saved as `2024`, `2024-03`, or `2024-03-15`, ready for future per-month/per-year charts.
- **Genre vs. tags:** a book's genre is what it's about (Fantasy, Mystery, History…). Audience and format are tags instead, the way libraries label them: Children's, Middle Grade, Young Adult, New Adult, Graphic Novel, Short Stories, Poetry, Audiobook and Series are always suggested in the tag dropdown.
- **Tags:** pick from a dropdown of tags you've already used, or type a new one and press Enter. Tags show as removable badges that slowly drift through pastel colors.
- **Notes:** write about a book on a torn-paper notepad, in your choice of six handwriting fonts (Caveat, Indie Flower, Homemade Apple, Nanum Pen Script, Shadows Into Light, Gloria Hallelujah) or a typewriter font (Special Elite). Notes and font are saved per book; notes on an existing book save as soon as you close the notepad.
- Image cropping on upload for covers and spine photos.

### Little delights in the edit window
- Clicking a star sparkles (every star sparkles for 5 stars).
- Marking a book **Read** pops confetti. (It leaves the Finished date alone; use its **today** button if you want today.)
- The preview book wiggles when you change its status or give it a new cover.
- A "reading ♡" sticker appears on the preview while a book's status is Reading.
- If a book has notes, a corner of notepad paper peeks out behind the preview. Hover and it slides up a little; click it and it pulls out and unfolds into the notepad. Closing the notepad tucks it back behind the book.
- New tags bounce in and removed ones shrink away; new books get a random cute example title and author as placeholders.
- Hover over the preview book and its cover lifts a little; click it and the cover swings wide open, and its library checkout card slides out of a library pocket with the title, author, page count and start/finish dates handwritten in your notes font, who it's on loan to (if anyone), plus a stamp for its status. Click to slide it back in.
  - **Writing dates on the card:** click any line under Checked out / Returned and write a date, in pretty much any form: `today`, `2024`, `3/2024`, `3/15/2024`, `Mar 2024`, `March 15, 2024`, `15 March 2024` or `2024-03-15`. Press Enter (or click away) to write it, Escape to leave it. The first line is the book's Started / Finished dates, so writing there fills in the form too. The blank lines below are for **re-reads**: each one is another time you read it. Clear both dates on a line and it goes away. A date the card can't read, or a return before its checkout, gets a little shake and a note at the bottom instead of being written. After four lines, the lines scroll. Like the rest of the edit window, it's saved when you save the book.
- All animations turn off if your device is set to reduce motion.

    - **Terminal:** a classic retro computer. The whole page sits inside a beige monitor (rounded screen corners, a power light and a little badge), with VT323 terminal lettering, scanlines, a `C:\>` prompt with a blinking block cursor, a `READY.` prompt, inverse-video shelf names and pressed buttons, double-line shelves, and box-drawing lines like a system-info readout (`┌──── DETAILS ────` section boxes in the edit and Settings windows, and `├─` tree branches on the main panel's labels). After dark the screen is a glowing green phosphor CRT, everything in shades of green; by day it's a paper-white screen with dark phosphor text.
    - **Typewriter:** a crisp typed manuscript. Bright white paper with faint ruled lines, true black ink, Special Elite typewriter lettering, paper-sheet panels with a faint margin line, black enamel title bars with a chrome rule, black platen shelves with chrome knobs, typed paper-slip shelf names, and dotted-leader section labels. Every button is a black typewriter key with cream letters in a chrome rim; a pressed key pushes down and flips to cream with black letters. The title types itself in, a letter at a time. After dark it's a black enamel typewriter on a desk by lamplight. **Typewriter machine** (in Settings while Typewriter is on; off by default) makes the main panel a sheet rising out of the machine: **Roller** puts the black rubber platen with knurled knobs along its bottom, the chrome paper bail with two rubber rollers just above, and the carriage-return lever out to the left; **Full front** adds a chrome typing scale and a clear card guide at the typing point. It's not shown on phones.
    - **Sunset:** a bold sunset from gold through orange, red and crimson to plum. A warm sky with the sun's glow and soft dunes rolling along the bottom of the page, a sunset-gradient title, rounded panels with a sunset rim, glowing horizon shelves, half-risen-sun shelf names, rounded Fredoka lettering, and soft pill buttons that glow when pressed. After dark it's dusk turning to night: deep indigo and plum, early stars, and the last of the sun glowing low behind the dunes.
    - **Plain** and **Plain Bold:** two quiet themes with no props or textures, each in six colours (Paper, Slate, Sage, Clay, Lilac, Navy; a **Colour** row shows in Settings while one of them is on, and the colour is kept when you switch between them). Plain is a plain Blueprint: squared panels with a crisp line and a soft offset shadow, Barlow Condensed capitals, a clean beam for a shelf and squared paper shelf labels. Plain Bold is a plain Loft: hard dark offset shadows, Oswald capitals, buttons with a hard bottom edge, a solid plank shelf and flat sign plates with a coloured edge. Pressed buttons and Add / Save are filled with the chosen colour. They borrow Blueprint's pup and Loft's moss sprout as critters.
    - In Library, Deco, Loft, Blueprint, Terminal, Typewriter, Sunset, Plain and Plain Bold the recent-colour swatches in the edit window are wax seals, diamonds, paint chips, typewriter keys or setting suns instead of hearts.
  - **Mode:** Light, Dark, or Match device (follows your computer or phone's setting). Works with every theme; the notepad, library card and sticky notes stay paper-colored.
  - **Bookshelf name:** rename the title at the top of the page.
  - **Handwriting:** the font new notes start in, and the font for library cards (or match each book's notes). Sticky notes are always in Nanum Pen Script.
  - **Custom genres:** see the genres you've added and remove any with its ×. If books still use it, you pick a genre to move them to first.
  - **Start new books as:** Want to Read, Reading, or Read.
  - **Shelf extras:** turn the shelf cat, dust, leaning books, the sticky-note pile, the reading critter and the pin board (Candy) on or off, and choose whether the owned seal and unread fading show nowhere, on the Series shelf only, or on every shelf.
  - **Calm mode:** turns off all the animations.
  - **Debug panel:** adds a **DEBUG** tab on the right edge of the page. Click it for buttons that make things happen right away, instead of waiting or using the browser console. Click a section's heading to fold it up (the panel remembers which are folded):
    - **Critter:** Feed 50, Next stage (hatches an egg, then grows it a stage), Previous stage (back a stage; a baby goes back to an egg), Take 50 (takes 50 pages off what it's eaten and out of its belly, which can drop it a stage), +1 day / +1 week (moves its clock forward, so hunger and running away happen as if that time passed), Full / Hungry / Starving, 1 day from leaving, Run away now, and New egg.
    - **Sticky notes:** New note (fills in the quick-add note with a random title and sticks it up), Make one fall, Stick all back up, and Delete one with a chosen exit (or a random one).
    - **Books:** Add a book (a random title, genre and status, scrolled into view), Remove debug books & notes (deletes only the ones the panel made), Delete one with a chosen exit (any theme's exit works in any theme), and Dust one (makes a Want to Read book heavily dusty).
    - **Backups:** Backup reminder (shows the reminder note now, whenever you last backed up) and Glow export (makes the Export button glow).
    - **Pins:** Earn one (a random pin you don't have), Earn all, and Reset pins (anything your shelf has already earned comes straight back).
    - **Other:** Wake the cat, Log 25 pages, Clear pages log, Confetti.

    **Deletes only ever touch books and notes the panel made** (with New note or Add a book), so your real library is safe. Deletes and exits only play for books and notes you can see, so scroll to one first. A line at the bottom says what happened, or why nothing did. Critter changes are real and stick.
  - **Start over:** delete the whole library (type DELETE to confirm), with an Export button right there to save a backup first. Optionally resets all settings too. The 3 starter books don't come back afterwards.
